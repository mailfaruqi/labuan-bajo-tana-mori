# Field photo slots

Drop provenance clean photographs here and they appear automatically. If a file is absent,
the slot stays hidden and the beat runs map only. Nothing is ever substituted in.

## Placeholders currently in use

The `.svg` files here are **placeholders**, not photographs. They are deliberately drawn as
diagrams so nobody can mistake them for documentary evidence of a real place. Replace each one
with a real photograph of the same subject and the story picks it up with no code change.

| `before-mud.svg` | The old track in the wet season |

Keep the same filename, or change `photos/...` in the beat definition in `index.html`.

## Agency supplied photography (national roads agency)

Photographs supplied by the **national roads agency** are welcome and useful: construction
progress, before and after, the trace under works. They carry one editorial condition.

> **They are the implementing agency's own documentation and must be credited as such.**
> Use `Courtesy: national roads agency, project documentation`.

This is not a slight on the material. The builder's camera points where the builder chose to
point it. It will show finished carriageway, not the sixteen houses that came down, and not the
flooded paddy at Nanganae. Presenting agency imagery as neutral documentation would let one of
the five institutional accounts quietly narrate the whole piece. Label it, and it becomes
evidence *of that account*, which is exactly what the story is about.

Where an agency photo sits beside contested material, pair it with the residents' account in the
same beat rather than letting it stand alone.

## Everything else

Only the survey team's own field photography goes here without an agency credit.

No stock imagery. No third party archival photos. No AI generated images. This is documentary
work about a real place and real people. A generic photo of *a* boat presented as *their*
crossing misrepresents the evidence, and a generated image would fabricate it outright.

## Before adding any photo, check

1. **Consent.** Did the person in frame agree to publication? Faces of identifiable villagers
   need explicit consent, especially given the intimidation documented in the report.
2. **Location.** Does the image reveal a specific home that ties to an identifiable individual?
   If so, crop it, widen it, or leave it out. Same rule as the coordinate generalisation.
3. **Caption honesty.** State what it actually shows and when. Do not let a photo of today's
   road imply the era before it.
4. **Credit.** Update the caption credit to the actual photographer.
