What this is

A survey, presented as a map

In April 2026 a field survey visited five villages along a new 25 kilometre road in western Flores, together with the institutions that planned, built, registered and benefited from it.

The survey asked one question. How is justice embedded in this infrastructure, both in the way it was decided and in what it produced.

This site presents that research as a map. It is just a documentary of a survey findings.

Research questions

What are the characteristics of infrastructure development in the Labuan Bajo to Tana Mori corridor, and what justice issues have emerged from it?

How is justice reflected in the planning process, across the deliberative, procedural and epistemic dimensions?

How do the impacts of the road reflect the distributive and restorative dimensions?

The framework

Five dimensions of justice

DistributiveAllocation of benefits and burdens.
ProceduralFairness and inclusiveness in decision making.
DeliberativeMeaningful participation with the ability to influence.
RestorativeAddressing past and ongoing injustice.
EpistemicRecognition of diverse forms of knowledge.

Method

Who was interviewed

5institutions: roads, public works, planning, land, zone operator
5villages: Nanganae, Cumbi, Kenari, Soknar, Golo Mori
1community advocate working with affected residents

Semi structured interviews, following an instrument built around the five dimensions above. Accounts are reported as given, including where they contradict one another.

Limits

What this evidence can and cannot show

This project is based on interviews, not large-scale surveys or statistical data. It reflects what people experienced, but it cannot show how common those experiences are across the whole community.

When interviewees described the same place differently, those differences were kept instead of forcing a single version. In places where no reliable map or ground truth was available, simple diagrams were used to illustrate what residents described. These are reconstructions from interviews, not surveyed locations.

The routes shown before the road was built are illustrative and represent residents' descriptions rather than exact historical routes.

Privacy and ethics

  • Institutions are identified by function only
  • Residents are not named, and no interview location is published at house level. Village markers are generalised
  • Photographs supplied by an agency are credited to that agency, so a reader can see whose view a picture represents.
  • Raw field data, including precise coordinates and contact details, is not published.
